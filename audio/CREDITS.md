# Ballroom quartet

Joseph Haydn — String Quartet in D major, Op. 64 No. 5 “The Lark”,
III. Menuetto. Allegretto. Performed by the **Musopen String Quartet**.

The composition is in the public domain. This recording is released under the
**Creative Commons Public Domain Mark 1.0**.

- Source: https://musopen.org/music/5261-string-quartets-hob-iii63-68/
- Wikimedia: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Haydn_StringQuartetInDMajorOp.64_JosephHaydn-StringQuartetInDOp.645H363Lark-03-MenuettoAllegretto.ogg
- Archive: https://archive.org/details/MusopenCollectionAsFlac

`cruise-ballroom-quartet.ogg` / `.mp3` are a local re-encode (loudness
normalization and short fades for looping), with an original light brush
and ride layer in 3/4 — no bass drum — so the on-stage drummer is heard
without covering the strings.

# Theatre — the nagauta for the kabuki

`cruise-opera-kabuki.ogg` / `.mp3` are **original work**, synthesised outright
by `tools/make_kabuki_score.py`. Nothing is sampled and nothing is borrowed:
there is no third-party recording in them, so they carry no third-party
licence.

They had to be written rather than found. A kabuki dance wants three minutes
of a hayashi and a nagauta ensemble that loops cleanly, and neither source the
ballroom's quartet came from has one:

- the 78 rpm nagauta discs on archive.org (including a complete *Kanjinchō*)
  carry no publication date and no licence, so none of them can be shown to be
  out of copyright;
- the Japanese traditional material on Wikimedia Commons is twenty-five second
  CC BY fragments of a festival float.

What the file contains: three shamisen in unison (Karplus–Strong with the
sawari buzz), a nohkan flute, ko-tsuzumi and ō-tsuzumi hand drums, the ō-daiko
behind the curtain, the hyōshigi clappers that call the house in, the tsuke
board beaten under each mie, and the drummers' kakegoe calls. It is laid out
in jo-ha-kyū, and `cruiseKabuki.js` cues the dance to the seconds printed at
the bottom of the generator — the mane flies on the drum because both read the
same table.

The music is in the miyako-bushi ("in") scale on D, which is traditional and
not anyone's property.
